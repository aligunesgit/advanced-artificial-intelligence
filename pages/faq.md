# Frequently asked questions

## Is this an introductory AI course?

No. Undergraduate AI and machine learning are prerequisites. The course emphasizes derivations, critical reading, and controlled experiments.

## Is it exclusively about large language models?

The D2L-based curriculum covers deep learning, computer vision, sequence modeling, attention, pretraining, GANs, reinforcement learning, and Gaussian processes. The course therefore has an explicit learning-centered focus within advanced AI.

## Do I need a GPU or paid API?

No. Required labs use small datasets, compact models, and finite environments. Discuss a CPU-scale substitute if a chosen project exceeds available compute.

## Is publication required?

No. A careful reproduction and a well-motivated extension are sufficient. Negative results are assessed on methodological quality.

## Are all labs graded?

No. Labs are ungraded learning activities. The only graded components are the midterm exam, final exam, and project.

## Are the schedule and grading final?

They are proposals. Credits, official dates, institutional rules, and final weights require instructor confirmation.

## Which textbook and implementation track do we use?

[Dive into Deep Learning](https://d2l.ai/index.html), using PyTorch. Required sections are listed in each module and in the reading map. Chapters 2–6 are mostly prerequisite review.

## What are the assessment weights?

Weights for the midterm exam, final exam, and project will be announced by the instructor. Sprint A is a formative project milestone; the project receives one grade from the final submission.
