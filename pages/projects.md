# Research project and assessment

Work individually or in pairs, subject to instructor confirmation. In a pair, maintain a contribution statement and shared run logs; oral defenses are individual. Select a narrow question that can be answered with available compute.

## Suggested project questions

- How do optimizer rankings change under equal hyperparameter-search budgets?
- Do skip connections improve optimization at approximately matched parameter counts?
- When does fine-tuning outperform frozen image representations on small data?
- How does context length affect a compact Transformer relative to a GRU?
- Which objective reduces mode collapse in a small synthetic GAN experiment?
- How does exploration affect Q-learning sample efficiency?
- How do kernel and noise choices affect GP predictive interval coverage?

These are project prompts, not claims of novel research. An approved alternative may connect two modules, but breadth must not compromise the experimental controls.

## Sprint A: proposal and baseline (15% of course grade)

Submit a 2–3 page proposal, selected D2L section and a related primary paper or precise algorithm specification, related-work table with at least three relevant sources, dataset and license, research hypothesis, metrics, compute budget, and a runnable baseline. Define the split, tuning budget, stopping criteria, and planned ablation before final experiments.

Rubric within this component: question and scope 25%, baseline correctness 35%, evaluation protocol 25%, feasibility and documentation 15%.

## Sprint B: final research package (30%)

Submit a 6–8 page main report, excluding references and appendices, plus code, environment specification, run commands, configurations, and compact results. Include an abstract, question, related work, methods, protocol, results, limitations, ethical considerations, and contribution statement. Compare a baseline with one justified extension and include at least one ablation. Use repeated runs where stochastic variation matters and explain your uncertainty method. Report compute and negative results.

Rubric: methodological correctness 30%, experimental evidence and ablation 30%, interpretation and limitations 20%, reproducibility 20%. Novelty and raw benchmark score are not grading criteria.

## Individual defense (10%)

Explain the core derivation, defend experimental controls, interpret an unexpected result, and identify a boundary of the evidence. Rubric: understanding 40%, defense of choices 30%, critical interpretation 30%.

## Individual labs (30%)

M1, M3, M5, and M7 are each worth 7.5% of the course grade. Per-report rubric: correctness 35%, experimental design 30%, evidence and interpretation 25%, reproducibility 10%. Other labs are formative and receive feedback.

## Paper critique and presentation (15%)

Submit a 2-page critique and present a primary paper agreed with the instructor. Identify the research question, central mechanism, evidence, assumptions, limitations, and a concrete follow-up experiment. Rubric: technical understanding 35%, evidence critique 35%, proposed experiment 20%, clarity 10%.

## Research integrity

Cite datasets, code, papers, and externally generated material. Disclose AI assistance and verify all referenced claims. Do not submit generated experiments or fabricated results. Separate model selection from final evaluation and record failed runs. Use public or appropriately authorized data. Institutional policies govern late work, accommodations, and misconduct; the instructor will publish these before assessment begins.

## Source-based project workflow

Start with one assigned D2L implementation, cite its exact section, reproduce a small baseline, and add one independently justified experimental change. Identify inherited code and student contributions. A book section is an implementation reference; the paper critique still requires a primary research paper.
