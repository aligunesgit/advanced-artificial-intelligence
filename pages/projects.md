# Research project and assessment

Work individually or in pairs, subject to instructor confirmation. In a pair, maintain a contribution statement and shared run logs. Select a narrow question that can be answered with available compute.

## Suggested project questions

- How do optimizer rankings change under equal hyperparameter-search budgets?
- Do skip connections improve optimization at approximately matched parameter counts?
- When does fine-tuning outperform frozen image representations on small data?
- How does context length affect a compact Transformer relative to a GRU?
- Which objective reduces mode collapse in a small synthetic GAN experiment?
- How does exploration affect Q-learning sample efficiency?
- How do kernel and noise choices affect GP predictive interval coverage?

These are project prompts, not claims of novel research. An approved alternative may connect two modules, but breadth must not compromise the experimental controls.

## Assessment components

The course grade consists only of **Midterm exam**, **Final exam**, and **Project**. Their weights, exam dates, and exam format are TBD and will be announced by the instructor. Labs, paper discussions, presentations, and oral defenses have no separate assessment component.

## Midterm exam

The midterm assesses the modules completed before the exam. It focuses on mathematical derivations, algorithm understanding, and experimental reasoning. The instructor will announce the exact scope and format in advance.

## Final exam

The final assesses the course learning outcomes, including advanced models and interpretation of experimental evidence. The instructor will announce the exact scope, whether it is cumulative, and the format in advance.

## Project

The project is one graded component developed across two milestones. Sprint A supports proposal and baseline development; Sprint B is the final submission. No separate lab report, paper critique, presentation, or oral defense is required as an additional graded activity.

### Sprint A: proposal and baseline development

Prepare a 2–3 page proposal, selected D2L section and a related primary paper or precise algorithm specification, related-work table with at least three relevant sources, dataset and license, research hypothesis, metrics, compute budget, and a runnable baseline. Define the split, tuning budget, stopping criteria, and planned ablation before final experiments. This milestone receives formative feedback and has no separate grade.

### Sprint B: final project submission

Submit a 6–8 page main report, excluding references and appendices, plus code, environment specification, run commands, configurations, and compact results. Include an abstract, question, related work, methods, protocol, results, limitations, ethical considerations, and contribution statement. Compare a baseline with one justified extension and include at least one ablation. Use repeated runs where stochastic variation matters and explain your uncertainty method. Report compute and negative results.

The project is evaluated on methodological correctness, experimental evidence, interpretation and limitations, and reproducibility. Detailed project marking weights will be announced by the instructor. Novelty and raw benchmark score are not grading criteria.

## Research integrity

Cite datasets, code, papers, and externally generated material. Disclose AI assistance and verify all referenced claims. Do not submit generated experiments or fabricated results. Separate model selection from final evaluation and record failed runs. Use public or appropriately authorized data. Institutional policies govern late work, accommodations, and misconduct; the instructor will publish these before assessment begins.

## Source-based project workflow

Start with one assigned D2L implementation, cite its exact section, reproduce a small baseline, and add one independently justified experimental change. Identify inherited code and student contributions. Use the book section as an implementation reference and related primary papers to support the project rationale.
